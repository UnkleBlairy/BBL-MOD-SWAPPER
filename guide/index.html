DIRTY HACK BUILD

A Teensy 4.1 runs the firmwares. The FM-1 is its screen, keys and speaker. Swap firmwares from a menu.

FOR PSYCHOPATHS ONLY. You solder to a Teensy, you flash your FM-1, you do it at your own risk. Not made or supported by M-VAVE or PJRC. You can always go back to stock (section 7).

1 What you need

PartNotes
Teensy 4.1Stock is fine. PJRC or any PJRC distributor. With or without pins.
microSD card32 GB or smaller, formatted FAT32.
USB-C cable to cutMust be a data cable (4 wires inside). Or buy a USB-C male 4-core pigtail: AliExpress. Plenty are 2-wire charge only: check it says 4 core / data.

Plus a soldering iron and a multimeter.

2 Wire it

  1. Cut the USB-C cable about 20 cm from the USB-C plug. Keep the plug end.
  2. Strip it. You want four wires: usually red 5V, white D-, green D+, black GND. Trim the bare shield back.
  3. Colours aren't a standard. Meter check: GND beeps to the plug's metal shell. Odd colours? Sort them out with a USB-C breakout before you solder.
  4. Solder them through the 5 host pads as drawn, from the top.
  5. Glue or tape the cable to the board so the joints don't flex.

Check 5V and GND twice before you power it. Swapped, they can kill the Teensy or the FM-1.

3 SD card

  1. Format the card FAT32.
  2. Unzip the SD folder from the Patreon zip and copy what's inside it to the top of the card: ROMS SAMPLES DATA STATES.
  3. Put your own samples (WAV) anywhere in SAMPLES.
  4. Card into the Teensy, label up.

The ports: the firmwares in the menu are files in ROMS. The Patreon zip already has them. New and updated ones go up on the PORTS PAGE:

  1. Press DOWNLOAD on a port, or DOWNLOAD ALL.
  2. Unzip it and copy its ROMS folder to the top of the card. The files go in with the ones already there.
  3. Each port is three files, NAME.R1 NAME.R2 NAME.R3. Keep all three.

4 Flash it

Everything happens in one page, in Chrome or Edge on a computer:

BBL MOD SWAPPER INSTALLER

  1. Teensy: plug it into the computer by micro-USB, choose the .hex from the Patreon zip, press Flash Teensy, press the Teensy's white button.
  2. FM-1: plug it straight into the computer (its own USB-C, no hub), switch it on, choose the .fwsc from the zip, press Install. Stay with it until it says Done.

Rather use PJRC's own app for the Teensy? Open the same .hex in Teensy Loader.

5 Hook up and play

  1. FM-1 into the cut USB-C cable on the Teensy.
  2. Teensy's micro-USB into the wall charger.
  3. Switch the FM-1 on. The screen stays black for about 4 s, then the splash, then the menu.

6 Which Teensy, which FM-1

  • Teensy 4.1 only. A 4.0 has no SD slot.
  • No PSRAM, one chip or two: all should work. PSRAM adds sample time. Tell us if yours doesn't.
  • FM-1: tested on Blairy's. There are older and newer FM-1s; if yours is different, please report it (section 9).

7 Back to stock

Open the installer, open Back to stock, choose M-VAVE's official FM-1_015.fwsc. The FM-1 is a normal FM-1 again. Nothing on the FM-1 itself was ever cut or opened.

9 Ports and testing

The firmwares are open-source FM-1 firmwares (most from the FM-1 Firmware Hub), ported to the Teensy. Every port is listed as UNCONFIRMED until someone other than its porter tests it.

  • All the ports: the PORTS PAGE, with direct downloads. So far: BBL Jam Box, JIANT FM, SLOOP and X0X, plus Felucca, built in. How to put them on your card is in section 3.
  • Tested one, or found a bug? Use the report form on the ports page. It goes straight to Blairy.

Port your own

You don't need to be a coder. An AI coding helper (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and the like) can do the porting:

  1. Pick an FM-1 firmware whose licence allows it (GPL, MIT and the like).
  2. Copy the AI PORTING PROMPT (one button on the ports page) into your AI helper, with the firmware's name and link.
  3. It builds the three .R1 .R2 .R3 files. Put them on your card and test them.
  4. Send it with the report form: choose New port and attach a zip. It goes on the ports page with your name on it.

10 Audio in (optional)

Add PJRC's Teensy Audio Shield and the Jam Box gets a live line in: play anything through it, record loops on its tracks, and sample straight to the card.

PartNotes
Audio ShieldPJRC Audio Adaptor Board, Rev D or D2 (the Teensy 4.x one). Rev C is for the old Teensy 3 and won't work.
3.5 mm stereo jackPanel or PCB jack, for the line in.
HeadersPins on the Teensy, long female sockets on the shield, so the Teensy plugs in on top.
PSRAMNeeded for recording loops: two chips = about 2 min 22 s of loop recording shared by 6 tracks, one chip = about 47 s.

Fit it

  1. The shield goes underneath, the Teensy plugs in on top, micro-USB ends together. That keeps the FM-1 cable on the Teensy's top host pads (section 2) free.
  2. Solder the jack to the shield's LINE IN pads (the right-hand group): tip to L, ring to R, sleeve to G. Left and right are mixed to mono.
  3. Nothing else to flash or set: the firmware finds the shield by itself. It uses pins 8, 18, 19, 20, 21, 23, 3.3V and GND.

Use it

  • Play through it: in the Jam Box, pick the INPUT machine on a track (PRESETS, past SMPL). The line in plays through that track's filter, drive and FX.
  • Record loops: the track's REC page (after SENDS): ARM, LEN up to 8 bars, ONE SHOT, REPEAT or LOOPER (overdubs every pass).
  • Sample it: the Sample Lab's recorder (SAMPLE LAB in the menu) saves up to 47 s to the card.
  • The shield's headphone jack hears the line in straight through.
  • The screen says NO LINE IN if it can't find the shield: check it's a Rev D and the pins are soldered.
